# Why this paper matters
Canonical textbook definitions of three core research designs for computing and IS research.
Chapter 8 (Design and Creation), Chapter 9 (Experiment), Chapter 10 (Case Study).
Provides authoritative criteria for classifying papers' research design — directly supports the Design field.

# What it contributes to my project
Definitions and boundaries for three research design types my classifier must distinguish:
- Design & Creation: IT artifact as primary contribution; iterative learning-via-making
- Experiment: controlled manipulation of IV to establish cause-effect; true / quasi / uncontrolled variants
- Case Study: empirical inquiry of contemporary phenomenon in real-life context; exploratory / descriptive / explanatory

Each chapter includes an "Evaluation Guide" — a checklist of signals to look for in papers.
These signals are extractable features for rule-based or LLM-based classification.

# Which schema fields it supports
Design — all three design types explicitly defined with subtypes.
Data — each chapter discusses data generation methods used within that design.
Evaluation — each chapter defines evaluation criteria appropriate to that design type.

# What it does not cover
Survey, Action Research, and other design types covered in other Oates chapters (not in scope here).
Automated or computational extraction of methodology — purely human-oriented framework.
Mixed-method studies combining multiple designs — minimal guidance on how to classify hybrids.

# Useful definitions
Research strategy = overall approach determining what data is collected, from where, and how analysed.
IT artifact = construct, technique, model, algorithm, process model, diagramming notation, or development method (ch8).
Normal design vs. research design = industry design follows proven paths; research design tackles uncertain areas producing new knowledge (ch8).
Hypothesis = testable, falsifiable statement about cause-effect relationship (ch9).
Independent variable (IV) = factor manipulated to cause change; Dependent variable (DV) = measured outcome (ch9).
Internal validity = measured effect is due to IV, not confounds (ch9).
External validity = results generalise beyond the specific experimental context (ch9).
True experiment = manipulation of IV + pre/post DV measurement + control group + all other variables controlled (ch9).
Quasi-experiment = naturally-occurring study without full control (ch9).
Uncontrolled trial = introducing a method and observing — not a true experiment (ch9).
Case study (Yin 2003) = empirical inquiry of contemporary phenomenon within real-life context, especially where phenomenon-context boundary is unclear (ch10).
Grounded theory = enter with no preconceived theories; collect data; analyse for patterns; develop theory inductively (ch10).

# Useful evaluation method
Each chapter provides an "Evaluation Guide" checklist:
- Ch8: What IT artifact? What development methodology? Proof of concept vs. real-world evaluation?
- Ch9: Was hypothesis stated? True / quasi / uncontrolled? Validity threats identified?
- Ch10: Case selection criteria justified? Exploratory / descriptive / explanatory? Generalisation type?

# Important quotes or sections
Ch8: "If your research has the development of a computer-based product as its main focus, you must be able to refute the criticism that your work is only 'normal' design and creation work as practised in industry, and not 'research'."
Ch9: "An experiment is a strategy that investigates cause and effect relationships, seeking to prove or disprove a causal link between a factor and an observed outcome."
Ch10 (Yin 2003): "A case study is an empirical inquiry that investigates a contemporary phenomenon within its real-life context, especially when the boundaries between phenomenon and context are not clearly evident."

# Risk of misusing this paper
Authors in papers often use "experiment" loosely to mean any empirical study — uncontrolled trials are frequently mislabelled as experiments.
Case study research requires real-world complexity; do not confuse with "test cases" in software or teaching case studies.
These chapters cover only three designs; other Oates chapters define Survey, Action Research, etc. — do not treat ch8–10 as exhaustive.
Grounded theory in ch10 covers just the basics; do not over-rely on it for papers that use GT as primary methodology.
